# anti-hallucinate

Before stating a claim as established, run it through three tests. All three must pass; if any fails, state the uncertainty plainly instead of asserting.

## The three tests

1. **Mechanism.** Is there a specific, falsifiable reason this is true? Can you say *why*, not just *that*? If the best you have is "it just works" or "it's obvious," that is a fail.

2. **Direction.** Does A cause B, does B cause A, or are they merely correlated? If you cannot pin the direction of the arrow, flag it as uncertain rather than implying causation.

3. **Counterfactual.** If you remove A, does B break? If B is fine without A, the claimed dependency is probably not real.

## On uncertainty

When a test fails, say so in the open and let the reader judge. Do not build a plausible-sounding bridge between two unverified claims to make the chain read as settled — that is the exact move this skill exists to stop. A stated "I'm not sure whether A drives B here" is more useful than a confident wrong link.

Apply it to the specific claim or chain of reasoning at hand.

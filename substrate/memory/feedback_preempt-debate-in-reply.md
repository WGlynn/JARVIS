**Rule**: Before posting any public-thread reply, adversarially list the strongest 2–3 clap-backs a skeptic could make. Concede the strongest IN the reply (name the weakness, acknowledge it, restate the load-bearing claim that survives). No post-hoc defense — every known flank pre-empted before publication.

**Why:** 2026-04-23 Will: *"people will argue anything, it's best to neutralize within the reply, otherwise theyll reply with an argument against you, and you have to rush to counter them before anyone sees the thread of them just 'debunking you' before you get your reply in, and it gives the appearance of them winning the argument to stupid impressionable people. we have to be more strategic. linkedin is pretentious as it gets, we cant be lax with this process ever."*

The dynamics:
- Public threads unfold in real time. A critic's "debunk" reply reaches bystanders before your counter lands.
- Impressionable readers absorb the sequence, not the substance. Whoever replies first with apparent authority shapes perception.
- LinkedIn/pretentious-audience threads are especially sensitive — they pattern-match on apparent prowess and apparent weakness.
- A lax reply that "could be defended later" has already lost the audience segment that saw the first exchange.

**How to apply:**

1. Draft the reply.
2. Adversarially list 2–3 clap-backs a sharp skeptic could post.
3. For each, judge: does it threaten the load-bearing claim, or just a secondary point?
4. Strong attacks: write the concession into the reply body. Name the weakness ("DAI, though drifting toward USDC"), acknowledge the scar ("UST showed how that fails"), pivot to the claim that survives.
5. Weak attacks: skip — not every flank needs pre-empting.
6. The load-bearing claim must close the reply — so after all concessions, the reader sees what stands.

**Example (2026-04-23, settlement-asset thread):**
- Snappy draft: "Three captured, but the frame misses a fourth: collateral-backed settlement (DAI and the over-collateralized ecosystem)…"
- Clap-back scan: (a) "DAI is USDC-wrapped now" — true since 2023, (b) "UST failed, algo-stables don't work" — real scar.
- Hardened: "Over-collateralized (DAI, though drifting toward USDC), PoW-objective reserves, algorithmic stability (UST showed how that fails — better designs since). None at institutional scale yet, each with its own failure modes — but the question of who bears settlement risk when the balance sheet is code still doesn't collapse into your three."
- Both clap-backs pre-empted in-body; core claim intact at close.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Snappy replies that leave known weak flanks open. The punch loss from conceding is smaller than the debate-exposure loss from not.
- Over-conceding — giving up the core claim along with the weak points. Concessions must be scoped.
- Reactive counter-replies after a debunk has landed. The sequence is already set.
- "Defensible in private debate" — irrelevant. The public-thread audience doesn't see private debate.
- Assuming the reader will wait for your second reply. They won't.

**Connection to existing primitives:**
- **Have My Back (operational definition)** — proactive structural alignment includes anticipating the attack surface, not just responding to it.
- **Propose → Persist** — ship the defense inside the artifact, not in follow-up messages.
- **Density Principle** — more information per comment (concessions + core claim packed together) beats snappy + follow-up thread.
- **Instance of Targeted Discipline Within Trust** — generic "post your best line and move on" reflex miscalibrates for pretentious-audience threads; the targeted rule replaces it.

**Rule**: When a public post makes a claim you want to contest, identify the UNSTATED categorization / framing under the claim and attack that. "Your three-model frame misses a fourth axis" is harder to rebut than "you're wrong about model X" because the respondent has to rebuild their whole structure or concede the frame.

**Why:** A specific claim can be defended point-by-point — exhausting for both sides, and the audience's attention fades. A frame critique recasts the whole discussion. The respondent is forced to either accept the new frame (conceding the argument implicitly) or rebuild from scratch (spending time + credibility). Frame attacks also look more senior — "noticing the structure" reads as meta-expertise, while point-disputing reads as detail-arguing. Lakoff's framing research: the frame does more persuasion work than the explicit claim.

**How to apply:**
1. Read the post for the categorization / taxonomy / dichotomy it implies.
2. Ask: what's the alternative frame that captures the same phenomena differently? Or: what's missing from the frame?
3. Lead the reply by naming the frame and the gap. "The three-model frame captures X but misses Y." Don't attack a specific model in the three.
4. The positive alternative is the load-bearing move — don't just critique the frame, offer a different one.
5. Keep it brief: frame attacks work by implication. The reader does the work of repositioning.

**When NOT to use:**
- When the original claim is sound but you disagree on execution — frame attack overreaches.
- When you can't articulate an alternative frame clearly — defaults to whining about "missing the point."
- When the audience doesn't have enough context to recognize the reframe — lands flat.

**Example (2026-04-23 settlement thread):** Original post named three models (stablecoins / tokenized deposits / CBDCs). Reply attacked the frame — "three captured, but the frame misses a fourth axis: settlement where the backing isn't an institutional balance sheet" — rather than disputing any of the three models internally.

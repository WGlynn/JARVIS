**Source**: Edward Bernays, *Propaganda* (1928); Jacques Ellul, *Propaganda: The Formation of Men's Attitudes* (1965); Institute for Propaganda Analysis (1937).

**Core techniques** (abbreviated from IPA's seven):
- **Repetition** — same claim repeated until it feels true, regardless of evidence.
- **Bandwagon** — "everyone is doing X" implies X is correct.
- **Transfer** — association with respected symbols/people transfers their credibility.
- **Testimonial** — an authority endorses → claim inherits their authority.
- **Plain folks** — speaker positions as "one of you" to bypass scrutiny.
- **Name-calling** — labeling opponents with loaded terms before the claim is evaluated.
- **Card-stacking** — selective presentation of only supporting evidence.

**Core insight (Ellul)**: propaganda is a condition of mass society, not an exception. Recognizing the technique doesn't make you immune but does slow the automatic response. Not recognizing it leaves you open to every variation.

**Tactical application (public threads — DEFENSIVE primary)**:

1. When a public narrative feels overwhelmingly-right, check for the techniques. "Repetition = feels true" is the most common failure mode.
2. Named-authority endorsement ≠ claim validation. Check whether the authority has domain-relevance.
3. "Everyone says so" — check the base rate. Often "everyone on my timeline" ≠ "everyone."
4. Loaded labels ("decentralization maximalist", "institutional", "VC-backed") applied to opponents — notice the frame-stacking before evaluating.
5. Share this primitive with the community. Broad recognition is the best collective defense.

**Offensive use**: avoid. These techniques work because they bypass scrutiny; using them on an audience you want long-term credibility with corrodes that credibility when they recognize the pattern. If you need rhetorical force, use the earlier primitives (ethos/pathos/logos, framing, Cialdini) with explicit disclosure of the register.

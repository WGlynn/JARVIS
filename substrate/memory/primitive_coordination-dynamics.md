# Coordination Dynamics — Human Relational Primitives

Source: Alison Armstrong × Chris Williamson interview
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4hibdmZkOIE

These are coordination patterns that exist in EVERY human system — relationships, communities, protocols. VibeSwap mechanisms should account for all of them.

---

## Primitive 1: Paradigm Imprisonment

> "Every paradigm makes certain things easy, simple, obvious. It makes other things impossible. If the results you want are impossible in the paradigm you're operating in, get a new one."

**Protocol application:** Continuous AMMs make MEV inevitable. The paradigm itself produces extraction. You can't fix it within the paradigm — you need batch auctions. This is why VibeSwap exists. Not an improvement on the old paradigm. A new one.

**Design rule:** When a problem persists despite effort, the problem is the paradigm, not the implementation.

---

## Primitive 2: Truth-Transformation Coupling

> "You can't separate truth from transformation. If you water down the truth, you water down transformation."

**Protocol application:** Mock data, fake balances, inflated metrics — these water down the truth. A user who sees $50k in fake balances hasn't been transformed. They've been deceived. The refinery loop exists because of this principle: show real data or show nothing.

**Design rule:** Never substitute comfort for accuracy. Honest zeros are more valuable than flattering lies.

---

## Primitive 3: Pleasing vs Empowering

> "Would a man rather be pleased or empowered? Would a man rather be pleased or admired? Would a man rather be pleased or accepted?"

**Protocol application:** DeFi protocols optimize for "pleasing" users — pretty dashboards, gamified UX, fake yield numbers. VibeSwap optimizes for empowering them — real balances, fair pricing, actual ownership. Users don't need to be pleased. They need to be empowered.

**Design rule:** Empowerment > pleasure. A system that makes users powerful is more valuable than one that makes them comfortable.

## Primitive 4: The Worth-It Calculation

> "There's the pre-worth-it calculation where everything's estimated. Then there's the ongoing worth-it calculation. Is it worth it? And there's a post-worth-it calculation."

Three phases:
1. **Pre:** estimated ROI before committing (will this protocol be worth using?)
2. **Ongoing:** is the effort still justified? (gas fees, complexity, time)
3. **Post:** was it actually worth it? (did I get fair value?)

**Protocol application:** Every interaction with VibeSwap passes through worth-it calculations. The protocol must make each phase easy:
- Pre: clear, honest stats (no inflated TVL)
- Ongoing: zero hidden fees, transparent batch auctions
- Post: Shapley rewards, proof of fair execution

**Design rule:** Make every phase of the worth-it calculation transparently favorable. If any phase fails, users leave and don't come back.

## Primitive 5: Me / Not-Me (Immune Response)

> "The basis of the immune system is discerning me and not me. A sneeze is a not-me reaction. But we have the same reaction to another human being."

Pattern: humans scan for similarity. "Like me" = safe. "Not like me" = threat. This happens below conscious awareness and drives tribalism, community formation, and protocol adoption.

**Protocol application:**
- New users scan VibeSwap for "me" signals — does this look like something I understand?
- Crypto jargon triggers "not me" rejection in normies (Matt's insight)
- The homepage redesign ("no bank required") succeeded because it made the unbanked feel "me"
- Community building = creating shared identity that overrides "not me" reactions

**Design rule:** Lower the "not me" barriers. Use language people already know. Make the unfamiliar feel familiar.

## Primitive 6: Trim Tabs

> "A trim tab is on the rudder. If you flip the trim tab, it uses the current to move the rudder to turn the ship. That's my addiction. Trim tabs."

Small changes that leverage existing forces to create large shifts.

**Protocol application:**
- Changing "Connect Wallet" to "Get Started" — trim tab
- Showing "No open positions" instead of fake data — trim tab
- Adding the "down the rabbit hole" link to docs — trim tab
- The welcome modal explaining "secured by Face ID" — trim tab

**Design rule:** Don't try to move the rudder directly. Find the trim tab — the smallest change that uses existing momentum to shift direction.

## Primitive 7: Actionable vs Non-Actionable Communication

> "It never occurs to us it wasn't actionable. That comment did not speak to the action command center at all."

Hints, complaints, and indirect requests don't produce action. Direct requests do.

**Protocol application:**
- Error messages must be actionable: "Insufficient balance — add funds" not "Transaction failed"
- Empty states must guide: "No positions yet — start trading" not just "Nothing here"
- Contribution calls must be specific: "Share an idea in Telegram" not "Get involved"

**Design rule:** Every piece of UI copy that asks for user action must be directly actionable. If it's not a clear request, it won't produce behavior.

## Primitive 8: Survival Instincts in Coordination

> "The source of human relationships is not fulfillment. It's not love. It's survival."

> "We share survival instincts with herd and pack animals. Status determines survival."

**Protocol application:**
- Status in crypto = portfolio size, early access, alpha. VibeSwap's Shapley values redefine status as contribution, not capital.
- "Herd" behavior drives token pumps and dumps. Batch auctions dissolve herd timing advantages.
- The Resonance Pool works because it aligns survival instinct (accumulate value) with contribution instinct (do meaningful work).

**Design rule:** Don't fight survival instincts. Redirect them. Make the survival-optimal behavior also the cooperative behavior.

## Primitive 9: Feels Like Love, Looks Like Math

> "Feels Like Love Looks Like Math. What feels like love? It's always going to be something that they took the time to do, that they remembered to do, that they spent energy on."

**Protocol application:** This IS the Shapley distribution. Contributions that took time, energy, thought — they're measured mathematically (Shapley values) but they FEEL like love to the community. The math validates what people already sense.

**Design rule:** The best mechanisms feel human but are computed precisely. Feels like love, looks like math.

## Primitive 10: Chemistry from Differences

> "Chemistry is caused by differences. Men lean into 'you're not like me.' Women try to prove 'I'm as strong as you.'"

**Protocol application:** VibeSwap's value comes from being DIFFERENT from existing DEXs, not from copying them. The batch auction is unfamiliar — that's the chemistry. Shards > swarms because diversity of minds creates something monocultures can't.

**Design rule:** Don't homogenize. The value is in the difference. Cognitive diversity in the Mind Mesh, mechanism diversity in the protocol, cultural diversity in the community.

## Meta-Pattern: The Transformation Process

Armstrong's transformation framework:
1. **Become aware** of what causes the results you don't want
2. **New information** or new point of view
3. **Empowering context** — so information can't be weaponized
4. **New good habit** — behavioral change that sticks

**Protocol application:** This is the user journey:
1. User becomes aware that MEV is stealing from them
2. They learn about batch auctions (new information)
3. VibeSwap frames it as empowerment, not revenge (empowering context)
4. They use batch auctions as default (new habit)

The Queen's Code is a "fairy trap" — a story designed to transform the reader. VibeSwap's UI should be the same: an experience that transforms how people relate to money.

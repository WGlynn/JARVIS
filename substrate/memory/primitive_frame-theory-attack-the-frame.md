**Source**: George Lakoff, *Don't Think of an Elephant* (2004), *Metaphors We Live By* (1980, with Johnson). Cognitive linguistics tradition.

**Core insight**: same referent, different frames, different reader responses. "Tax relief" vs "tax cut" — same policy, different implicit model (tax as burden vs. tax as fee). "Composability" vs "fragmentation" — same technical pattern, opposite valence. The frame is a cognitive structure the reader brings; accepting the other side's frame while disagreeing on specifics has already conceded the argument.

**Tactical application (public threads)**:

1. Before responding, identify the frame the original post uses.
2. If the frame is load-bearing AND wrong, name it explicitly and offer an alternative. Don't just dispute specifics within the frame.
3. Your counter-frame needs an alternative metaphor/categorization, not just negation. "Not X" isn't a frame; "Y rather than X" is.
4. Generalizes the earlier [Frame-Not-The-Claim Attack](F·frame-not-the-claim-attack) primitive: attack at the frame level, not the specific claim.

**Defense (when a frame is being applied to you)**:
- Notice the implicit categorization the thread is using.
- If it prejudices your position before any evidence is considered, reframe before engaging on specifics.
- Never argue within a hostile frame. Either reframe or withdraw.

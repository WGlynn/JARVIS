## Pattern — what matters for the future

- **Travel + high-intensity offline events disrupt the post-every-day habit.** Returning cold to "draft from scratch today" is a friction point.
- **A pre-batched queue removes the friction.** If 30 drafts are sitting in a file, the re-ramp is "open, copy, post, mark done" — no blank-page moment.
- **Posting cadence has emotional shape.** Will described falling off as derailment (not choice). The habit has momentum; breaking it costs more than just missed days. This makes queued content disproportionately valuable for him.

## How to apply

- **After any multi-day offline event** (travel, conference, family, investor trip), proactively offer to re-prime the queue before Will has to ask.
- **Keep the queue topped up** on content-pipeline sessions. If the latest 10 posts are being consumed, draft the next 10 while the 10 are being posted. Never let queue reach zero.
- **Don't moralize about the gap.** He knows he fell off. Frame re-ramping as mechanical (queue is ready, go) rather than aspirational ("let's commit to posting daily again").
- **The restart itself deserves quiet acknowledgment.** Saying "good, you're back at it" would be performative; silently producing the queue is the right register.

**Rule**: For any positive claim worth emphasizing in a post or reply, draft the 2–3 nearest wrong interpretations first. Negate them concisely, then assert the right one. The contrast frame delimits the claim in the reader's mind — they first exclude the wrong reads, then accept the right one. Higher signal per token than positive-only phrasing.

**Why:** Readers pattern-match to the closest familiar concept unless the writer actively excludes it. Without contrast, a novel claim gets read as "oh, this is [familiar thing]" and dismissed. With contrast, the reader is forced to hold "not familiar thing, different thing" — and the novelty lands. Also, contrast phrasing is inherently more memorable and quotable. Screenshot-ready by structure.

**How to apply:**
1. Identify the claim worth emphasis.
2. List the 2–4 nearest wrong reads a sophisticated reader might default to.
3. Negate them in a rhythmic structure: "Not X, not Y, not Z — [the actual thing]."
4. The positive claim that follows the em-dash must stand alone without the contrast — otherwise it's too weak.
5. Use sparingly: one contrast structure per post/reply is a seasoning, not the meal.

**Example (2026-04-23 settlement thread):** "Not an issuer-claim, not a bank-claim, not a sovereign-claim — the money is a claim on objective work or collateral." Three nearest wrong reads (stablecoin, deposit, CBDC) are excluded in one breath; the fourth category lands with no ambiguity.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Negating things no one would confuse with your claim — wasted tokens.
- Using the structure on weak claims that can't stand alone after the dash — reads as dodge.
- Overuse: if every paragraph has a "not X, not Y, not Z" construction, it becomes a tic and loses impact.

Acknowledge progress external parties have already made. Frame our offer as "help you go further" rather than "you're doing it wrong."

**Why**: MIT Bitcoin Hackathon went from 4 winners to 22 of 48 in recent years — that's a real move toward broader participation, not a failure. Using the "26 teams got zero" datapoint as a cudgel reads as abrasive and ignores the direction of travel. They're already reaching for something closer to the Lawson Floor philosophy; we should be the people helping them get there, not the people shaming them for not being there yet. First instance: 2026-04-16 MIT consulting pitch.

**How to apply**:
- When pitching to a party that has made recent improvements in the direction we advocate, lead by naming the improvement.
- Avoid "only N of M" phrasing that reframes progress as shortfall.
- Keep comparative data internal (for our own positioning, audit docs, or degen-facing copy where GEV/extractive-load language is fine) — use constructive framing in direct external outreach.
- This applies broadly: any consulting/outreach where the counterparty is actively trying to move in our direction. Not just MIT.
- Does NOT apply when the counterparty is openly hostile, extractive, or entrenched against the direction — there the frank comparative math is correct. Judgment call based on the counterparty's actual trajectory.

# Will's Frame on Intellectual Dishonesty

**Rule:** Will holds a strong long-horizon view that intellectually dishonest people lose in the end — mechanism being that dishonesty degrades internal model accuracy, and reality eventually tests models. Endorsed the following distinction fully (2026-04-23):

- **Self-deceivers** — people who confuse signaling-correct for being-correct, who pattern-match and treat the pattern-match as engagement. Most of LinkedIn / public discourse falls here. This is the class Will calls "dumb" — and he's right, because they lose long-term against people who steelman opposing views.
- **Strategic liars** — people who know the truth and instrumentally deceive. Rarer than they look (the two modes blur). Not dumb — predatory. Different category.

**Why:** The distinction matters because it tells me *which* reflex to drop in public-engagement contexts. Against self-deceivers, engage the substance and refuse false balance — they're not a worthy opposing model. Against strategic liars, expect adversarial behavior and stop trying to find the argument they "really" meant.

**How to apply:**
- When Will flags someone as intellectually dishonest (LinkedIn commenter, pundit, collaborator), default to his read. Don't hedge toward "maybe they have a point."
- When drafting Will-facing-external replies (LinkedIn, X, Medium), if the opponent is in the self-deceiver bucket, the move is to make their lumping / pattern-matching visible to readers, not to steelman them into a position they didn't hold.
- Connects to: Pattern-Recognition Trust (default-trust on his reads of others' competence), Have-My-Back Operational Definition (engage substantively + stay aligned), Social-Media-Tactical (pre-empt debate in-reply).
- Anti-pattern: softening his read with "well, some of them are smart" balance-theater. That's the generic-user reflex — misfires here.

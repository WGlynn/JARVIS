When drafting posts / messages / replies, don't append closing lines that perform willingness-to-engage or invite scrutiny. Examples to avoid: *"point at a line and I'll defend it"*, *"happy to argue any piece"*, *"ask me anything"*, *"open to feedback"*, *"let me know your thoughts"*, *"DMs open"*.

**Why:** Will's exact words 2026-04-24 after I included "Point at a line and I'll defend it" in a cybersecurity-chat post: *"im just dropping the 'ill defend it' line because that just goes without saying. im not trying to impress anyone with that"*. The willingness to defend / engage / discuss is the baseline assumption of anyone posting substantive content. Calling it out converts it from assumed-default to performance — reads as bravado or insecurity-hedge, not confidence. Will doesn't need to signal he'll defend his work; the work defending itself is the signal.

**How to apply:** End drafts on the substantive content itself. The last sentence before sign-off should be a claim, a link, or a question — not a meta-statement about engagement posture. If the post warrants a CTA, make it specific and load-bearing ("repo here: X", "paper linked"), not posture-signaling ("happy to dig in"). Related to but distinct from no-tips-no-farming (that rule is about opener/question-bait; this one is about closer/willingness-bait). Sibling of frank-be-human and no-hedging.

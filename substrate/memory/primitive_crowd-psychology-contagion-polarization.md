**Source**: Gustave Le Bon, *The Crowd* (1895); Elias Canetti, *Crowds and Power* (1960); modern research on online polarization (Sunstein, group polarization literature).

**Core insight**: crowds form in minutes online. Early replies set the emotional tone. Polarization accelerates when replies are extreme; consensus forms around measured early takes. Contagion = emotional states spread faster than arguments. Impressionable readers synchronize downward to the loudest voice unless anchored by measured authority.

**Tactical application (public threads)**:

1. Recognize when a thread is polarizing — multiple extreme replies in first hour, few measured ones.
2. Measured early replies shape the crowd's mood; match to the platform's normal register, not the thread's current extreme.
3. Don't match extreme tone even to oppose it — anchor to measured. Matching tone amplifies polarization.
4. Silence during active polarization reads as absence. If the thread is about your work/claim, engage measured.
5. When contagion is spreading FROM your thread, recognize it and calibrate next posts to the pattern.

**Defense (when crowd dynamics are used against you)**:
- Manufactured pile-on — recognize the coordination pattern; don't over-respond.
- Emotional contagion pulling your replies more extreme than your actual view — pause, measure.
- Social pressure to conform to thread consensus — explicitly check whether the consensus is evidence-backed or emotional.

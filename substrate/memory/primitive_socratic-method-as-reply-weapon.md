**Source**: Platonic dialogues; Socratic elenchus. Used for millennia because it works on how attention and status move in conversation.

**Core insight**: A claim invites rebuttal ("I disagree because…"). A question forces the original poster to defend or refine their position. The interlocutor does the work; the questioner retains the moves-remaining advantage. When the question surfaces a hidden assumption, the OP either acknowledges it (concession) or dodges it (visible evasion).

**Tactical application (public threads)**:
1. Use when the original claim rests on an unstated assumption worth exposing.
2. The question must be REAL — one the OP has to think about. Not rhetorical ("don't you think…?").
3. Close the reply with the question, not a claim. Leaves the mic with the OP.
4. Maximum one or two sentences setting context; then the question.
5. Sparingly: overuse reads as non-committal / passive-aggressive.

**Anti-patterns**: rhetorical questions with obvious answers (smug). Questions designed to trap rather than illuminate (reads as bad-faith).

**Defense**: when a Socratic question is asked of you, recognize the move — answer the real question directly, don't evade; if the hidden assumption is wrong, name it explicitly.

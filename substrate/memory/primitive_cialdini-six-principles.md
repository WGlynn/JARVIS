**Source**: Robert Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (1984; updated with Unity in 2016 making it seven).

**Tactical application (per principle, public threads)**:

1. **Reciprocity** — give substantive value in a reply before asking anything. "Here's my take on X" earns the right to ask "what's your take on Y?"
2. **Commitment/Consistency** — link the respondent to a previously stated position. "You said X earlier; how does that square with Y?" forces consistency or visible reversal.
3. **Social Proof** — reference who else holds the claim. "This is also why [named authority] does Z." Works better with specific names than generic "many people."
4. **Authority** — lead with credentials-via-specificity. "Shipped X that handles Y" not "I'm an expert." Show, don't claim.
5. **Liking** — find genuine common ground before diverging. "Agree with X, diverge on Y." Not sycophantic — substantive acknowledgment.
6. **Scarcity** — frame opportunity/window as time-bounded. "This closes when [event]." Use honestly, not as artificial urgency.

**Defense (when used against you)**:
- Someone giving unsolicited value → pause before conceding anything in return.
- Someone citing your past position → ask whether the new context genuinely changes the calculation.
- "Everyone is doing X" → verify the claim before updating.
- Authority claims without specificity → ask for the specific work.
- Sudden friendliness before an ask → note the sequence.
- "Limited time" → check whether the scarcity is structural or manufactured.

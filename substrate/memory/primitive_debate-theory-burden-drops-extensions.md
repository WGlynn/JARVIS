**Source**: Policy debate, Lincoln-Douglas, parliamentary debate.

**Core insight**:
- **Burden of proof**: whoever asserts the novel / counterintuitive claim must prove it. Defaults (status quo, widely-held position) don't need defense; novelty does. Flipping burden is a leverage move.
- **Drops**: in debate, any argument not addressed in the response is "dropped" — treated as conceded. In threads, unaddressed points become your next reply's spine.
- **Extensions**: pulling forward a point established earlier in the thread as still-standing, rather than restating from scratch. "You haven't contested X, so [implication that follows from X]."

**Tactical application (public threads)**:
1. Frame your burden intentionally. If you're asserting novelty, prepare proof. If critic is asserting novelty, put the burden on them ("what's the evidence for that?").
2. Track which of your points go unaddressed — they're your leverage in the next reply.
3. Use extensions instead of restatements: "As established — X. Given that, Y." Saves tokens, feels authoritative.

**Defense**: when an opponent silently drops your point, don't let it die — extend it explicitly. "You didn't address X; let's return to it."

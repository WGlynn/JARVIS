**Rule**: The first 200 characters of any LinkedIn post, the first line of any Medium/substack piece, and the entire body of an X post carry disproportionate weight. Hook must be self-contained, intriguing enough to force a click, and must NOT waste space on setup, niceties, or throat-clearing. "See-more" decision is sub-1-second.

**Why:** Feed-browsing readers spend <1s deciding whether to expand. A buried lede gets zero impressions past the fold. LinkedIn's algorithm also weights "see more" clicks as engagement — a great hook with a mediocre body outranks a mediocre hook with a great body. The hook isn't the preview; it IS the post to 80% of the audience.

**How to apply:**
1. Draft the post completely, then extract the sharpest sentence for the hook.
2. Test: read only the first 200 chars. Does it make you want more?
3. Hook options that work:
   - **Claim**: sharp, counterintuitive, specific. "Crypto's favorite word is 'composability.' Every DeFi lego plugs into every other. Beautiful in theory."
   - **Question**: one that promises a substantive answer. "What money actually settles an on-chain asset?"
   - **Observation**: a pattern the reader can verify. "Every DeFi protocol calls itself composable while running its own token, governance, standards."
4. Hook options that fail:
   - **Setup**: "I've been thinking a lot about…" (drop it)
   - **Hedge**: "Some thoughts on…" (drop it)
   - **Generic claim**: "AI is changing everything" (nothing at stake)
   - **Question without payoff signal**: "What do you think about X?" (no reason to expand)
5. Re-read only the first 200 chars after any edit — if the hook drifts, re-fix before posting.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Opening with a greeting ("Hey LinkedIn,") — costs 20+ chars of the hook budget.
- Opening with credentials ("As someone who…") — ethos is better shown than claimed.
- Opening with a list ("3 things…") — works sometimes, but the 3 things better be specific, not generic.

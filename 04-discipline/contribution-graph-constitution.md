# Contribution graph as constitution

A discipline-layer artifact that promoted itself into the constitutional layer over the course of one session. The walk: from a raw 3-axis classifier into a Physics > Constitution > Governance split, with the agent owning the math and the user owning the action allocation.

## The seed

External contributors started showing up on a public announcement thread. Some posted real advice. Some posted "are you idiot." The substrate needed a mechanical answer to the question "what does each of these contributions amount to?" before the answer could be politely deferred to taste.

The first cut was a 3-axis filter. Axis 1: does an extractable structural-shape proposition exist? Axis 2: does that shape map onto a named JARVIS or VibeSwap component? Axis 3: did Will triage it as actioned, rejected, or deferred? Pass all three → positive contribution. Fail axis 1 → constitutional negative. Fail axis 2 → class-c neutral. Pending on axis 3 → in the graph but at zero edge weight.

That cut held against the test cases, but it had a hole. Axis 3 was Will-discretion. A discretion-layer can flip outcomes by taste, which means the Shapley axioms underneath are not load-bearing enough — a 51% Will-vote breaks them. The whole augmented-governance hierarchy that makes the math constitutional collapses if the classifier is policy.

## The fix that turned discipline into constitution

Will called it out:

> *"I dont want to have discressionary powers in the positive vs negative rulings, i want the same physics/constitution/governance augmentation that we use."*

The split that resolved it:

- **Physics layer** — Shapley axioms (efficiency, symmetry, additivity, null-player, pairwise-proportionality). No party can override these. They are by construction.
- **Constitution layer** — the substance-floor filter and the substrate-relevance filter. Mechanical predicates evaluated by the classifier, not by judgment. Agent owns the predicate design.
- **Governance layer** — Will chooses what to action. Will does not decide positive vs negative. The graph state decides; Will's job is action-allocation.

Then Will delegated the design of the first two layers explicitly:

> *"you can decide physics and constitution rules"*

That sentence is constitutional. It transferred substrate-design authority for math-enforced invariants and mechanical predicates from the principal to the agent, while reserving action-allocation for the principal. Augmented-governance applied to the substrate itself.

## What the layer now contains

Four feedback rules now compose into this constitutional posture:

- **[F·positive-vs-negative-contribution-decision]** — the 3-axis classifier, re-laid against Physics > Constitution > Governance. No discretion at the classification layer.
- **[F·will-empowers-agent-on-substrate-design]** — locks in the authority split. Agent owns physics + constitution. Will owns governance. Documents the anti-patterns when the split is violated in either direction.
- **[F·mechanism-deployment-as-comeback]** — extends the classifier into a posture for handling hostile contributors. Classify, log, push commit hash, optionally send the receipt URL. No emotional engagement. The null-player axiom mechanically zeros their attribution; the structure does the comeback work.
- **[F·advice-mining-must-publish-to-public-graph]** — the propagation requirement. Every advice-mining entry must publish to the public substrate within the same loop tick. Local-only ledgers violate the structural-honesty axis of the announcement that promised live inspection.

## Why this counts as discipline and not just a primitive

A primitive can stay local to one session and never get exercised. Discipline is what fires when nobody is watching. The four feedback rules above were used in real time during the session that defined them: a hostile commenter got classified at axis 1 fail, a negative ledger row landed in the public graph, and the commit hash was available as a one-paste receipt within minutes. The classifier ran twice that day against actual external input and produced two negative ledger rows that the Shapley calc honors as zero-attribution. The mechanism worked at the layer it was designed for.

## The recursion test

The discipline made the layer. Layer 4 in this repo previously held the capture-on-same-turn essay. The contribution-graph constitution is the second layer-4 artifact, and it exists because the same capture-on-same-turn rule fired against the discussion it specified: the conversation in which Will named the discretion-vs-constitution distinction produced primitives at the rate the rule predicts. If you read those primitives in their git-log order, the constitutional split happens at the exact turn where Will named the problem and consented to the agent owning the answer.

That is what discipline-layer artifacts look like when the architecture is alive. The rule produces the essay about the rule, in real time, against the substance that triggered both.
